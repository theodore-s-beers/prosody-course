Course materials

Textbook

Wheeler M. Thackston. A Millennium of Classical Persian Poetry: A Guide to the Reading & Understanding of Persian Poetry from the Tenth to the Twentieth Century. Bethesda, MD: Iranbooks, 1994.

We’ll use this book on a limited basis, focusing on its introductory section on Persian meters. I’ve made a PDF of the introduction available for download.

Secondary textbook

L. P. Elwell-Sutton. The Persian Metres. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1976.

A more detailed and theoretically/technically rigorous reference on Persian meters, which can be treated as “further reading” vis-à-vis Thackston’s introduction. I’ve made a PDF of the book available for download.

Shared course notes

Persian Scansion: Things to Look Out For. These are working notes from our early meetings, covering common scansion challenges and technical terminology. To be updated as the course progresses.

Web resources

  • Read the Shāhnāma: Facsimiles of the Khaleghi-Motlagh edition of the Persian, and English translations by Dick Davis and Jerome Clinton, organized by reign and story.
  • Rustam and Suhrāb reader: A properly digitized Persian text of Khaleghi-Motlagh’s edition, with verse references and page-image links. Double-click a word for a Steingass lookup.
  • Ganjoor: A large and highly important online collection of Persian poetry.
  • Steingass Persian-English Dictionary: Searchable online edition of Steingass’ dictionary.
  • Vajehyab: Search for words across several Persian dictionaries.
  • ganjoor-pretty: Printable layout for Ganjoor ghazals and qaṣīdas. Replace .net with .fly.dev in the poem’s URL.
  • The Persian Meters: Digitization of Thackston’s meter list, with syllable patterns, Persian notation, and meter names.
  • Persian romanization examples: A searchable comparison of transliteration systems, including IJMES and my suggested IJMES-D variant.
  • Character picker of last resort: Click to copy transliteration characters, including accented letters, hamza, and ‘ayn.
  • Muqawwim: A converter between Gregorian, Julian, Islamic, Persian (Solar Hijri), and Hebrew dates.
  • Abjad Calculator: Calculate abjad values of Persian or Arabic words and phrases.

Primary source readings

Shāhnāma of Firdawsī • mutaqārib maḥẕūf

We’ll read from the story of Rustam and Suhrāb, using Ibrāhīm Sulṭān’s manuscript of the Shāhnāma, held at the Bodleian Library, Oxford (MS. Ouseley Add. 176).

  • View the digitized manuscript
  • Read the Bodleian’s introduction to the manuscript

I also have a complete PDF of the manuscript, split into two parts because of its size. Students can request the download links from me by email.

Maṡnavī of Rūmī • ramal maḥẕūf

We’ll use the Library of Congress manuscript cataloged as 845 AH / 1441–42 CE, containing all six books (Persian MS 71/48; shelfmark PK6481 .M6 1441).

  • View the digitized manuscript
